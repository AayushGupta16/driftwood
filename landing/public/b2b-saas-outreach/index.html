B2B SaaS outreach: how to do cold outbound in 2026

By the driftwood team · July 2026

Guide · Updated July 2026

B2B SaaS outreach is cold outbound where the buyer is a software company, and usually so are you. This is a practical guide for founders and early growth hires running it themselves: targeting before copy, how to write the messages, what proof-of-work outreach looks like, and the funnel numbers to plan on.

On this page: What's different Targeting first Writing the message Follow-ups Proof of work Funnel numbers FAQ

What makes B2B SaaS outreach different

Your prospect runs outbound too, and filters accordingly.

Who you are writing to. In most industries, cold outbound competes with other salespeople. In B2B SaaS it competes with people who run outbound themselves. Your prospect is a founder, an engineer, or an operator at a software company; they have sent cold email, and they recognize the templates, the merge tokens, and the manufactured familiarity, because they have used them.

What AI changed. AI writing tools made this worse. A researched-sounding sequence now costs nothing to produce, so a SaaS buyer's inbox is full of messages that mention their company and say nothing about it.

The filter. The result is a filter those buyers now run by default: could this exact message have been sent to anyone else? If yes, it gets deleted, usually without being consciously read.

The way through. That filter is the constraint the rest of this page works around. Nothing below is a trick for getting past it. The only reliable way through is a message that could not have been sent to anyone else. That is expensive to produce, which is exactly why it works.

Targeting before copy: ICP, list quality, bounce hygiene

Most failed campaigns die in the list, not the copy.

The order. Most outbound advice starts with the email. Most failed campaigns die earlier than that, in the list. Work in this order, and do not skip ahead.

Define an ICP narrow enough to change the message. Stage, team shape, and the trigger that makes your problem current for them this quarter. The test: reading the ICP tells you what the first line of the email should mention. If it does not, it is a market description, not an ICP.

Verify every address before it enters a campaign. Database exports and scraped lists decay fast. Keep bounces under 2%; above that, stale addresses damage sender reputation, and every later send pays for it. The thresholds and the studies behind them are on the cold outbound benchmarks page.

Fix deliverability before touching copy. A reply rate means nothing if the emails sit in spam. If replies are under 1%, work the diagnostic order on the benchmarks page: bounce rate, then a placement test, then and only then the copy. Most sub-1% campaigns are a list or deliverability problem, not a copy problem.

Small and specific beats large and plausible. For SaaS specifically, a hundred companies that match the trigger will out-reply a thousand that match the industry code, and the smaller list is the only one a founder has time to research properly anyway.

How to write a cold email subject line

Two to four plain words, and no cleverness.

Short, specific, and plain. Two to four words that name the thing the email is about: "your onboarding flow", "the checkout bug", "QA without a QA team". The subject line has one job, which is to not look like a campaign. Internal email has plain subjects, so plain subjects get opened.

The campaign signals. Clever subjects, questions with the prospect's first name in them, and fake "Re:" threads are all campaign signals now, and your prospect sends campaigns for a living.

Do not optimize for opens. Open tracking has been unreliable for years, which is covered on the benchmarks page. Write the subject for the person who will already open it, as the label above your first line in the inbox preview.

How to start a cold email

With the observation, not with yourself.

The first line. The first line is the whole message in miniature: it should contain something you could only write after looking at their product, their docs, their changelog, or their job posts.

The test. "You are hiring three backend engineers and no QA" passes it. "I came across your company and was impressed" does not. Neither does "congrats on the raise", which AI sequences now generate automatically off funding announcements.

No introductions. Skip the introduction of yourself and your product. The prospect will read your signature and your domain if the first line earns it. "I hope this finds you well" costs a line and announces a template.

How long should a cold email be

As long as the specific thing you have to say, then stop.

The usual advice. Under 100 words, and it is roughly right, but length is a symptom rather than a rule. The email should be exactly as long as the specific thing you have to say: the observation, why it matters to them, one ask. If that takes 60 words, stop at 60.

What kills replies. Not word count, padding: the paragraph about your company, the second feature, the list of logos. Every sentence that could be deleted without losing the specific point should be deleted.

One ask. Keep it to one ask. Two asks usually gets neither answered.

How to write a follow-up that is not just a bump

A follow-up earns its send by adding something.

Why bumps fail. "Just bumping this to the top of your inbox" adds nothing and reads as automation, because it usually is.

What earns the send. A new observation, a sharper version of the ask, a piece of work you did since the first email. If the first email showed you looked at their product, the follow-up can show you looked deeper.

How many, how spaced. Two or three follow-ups per thread is enough on email. Space them out: a few days after the first send, then about a week. Send them in the same thread, so the prospect sees the original message above your follow-up instead of a context-free nudge.

How many touches before you stop

Four to six across both channels, then work upstream.

The number. Across email and LinkedIn together, four to six touches over two to three weeks, then stop. This is a judgment call, not a measured law. The public datasets do not agree on an optimal touch count, and the ones that exist come from volume senders, so treat any precise answer you read with suspicion.

The reasoning. Simpler than the data. By the sixth unanswered touch, the reply you are most likely to get is an annoyed one, and the prospect you are most likely to convert is one you have not contacted yet.

What silence means. Silence after a good sequence is information: it usually means the list is wrong or the message did not show enough work. The fix is upstream, not an eighth touch.

What a proof-of-work message looks like

The part AI sequences cannot imitate.

The ceiling on copy. Everything above raises the floor. It does not solve the core problem, which is that well-written, researched-sounding copy is now exactly what AI sequences imitate. The way out is to put something in the message that cannot be templated: work.

What it looks like. A proof-of-work message carries evidence that someone spent real effort on this specific prospect. For a SaaS company, the strongest version is your own product already working on the prospect's business: their site, their data, their use case, before the first call. The message stops claiming a demo would be worth their time and includes the demo. We call this demo-led outbound.

Realistic funnel expectations

Plan with public numbers, not with a vendor's case studies.

The spread. The short version, with named sources and links on the cold outbound benchmarks page: at average quality and at scale, cold email replies now measure under 1% (Belkins' 2026 study of 7.5 million sends measured 0.45%). Small, carefully targeted, heavily personalized campaigns still measure between 5% and 18% in the public studies.

Common questions

The questions founders ask about the channel itself.

Product questions about driftwood are on the FAQ.

How do you do B2B SaaS outreach?

In this order: define an ICP narrow enough to change the message, build a verified list and keep bounces under 2%, fix deliverability before touching copy, write short messages that prove you looked at the specific company, follow up two or three times with something new each time, and stop after four to six touches. The common failure is inverting the order and polishing copy on top of a bad list.

What is a good reply rate for B2B SaaS cold email?

Sopro's State of Prospecting 2026 puts most campaigns between 1% and 5%, and Instantly's 2026 benchmark guide calls 5-10% good and 10-15% excellent. At scale and average quality, measured replies fall under 1%. If you are below 1%, check bounce rate and deliverability before the copy. The full set of studies is on the cold outbound benchmarks page.

Does cold outreach still work for B2B SaaS in 2026?

Yes, with a widening spread. The average keeps falling as AI-written volume grows: Belkins measured 0.45% average replies across 7.5 million emails sent in 2025. The top end holds: Woodpecker's data shows advanced personalization replying at roughly 17-18% against 7-9% without. The channel rewards messages that show real work and punishes everything else a little harder each year.

Should you use email or LinkedIn for B2B SaaS outreach?

Both, from your own accounts. In the public platform datasets, LinkedIn message replies run about double cold email replies in the same studies, and connection accepts average roughly 19-30%. Email scales further and is easier to run deliverability checks on. For founder and executive targets, a LinkedIn message from a real profile tends to get read; for volume and threaded follow-ups, email is the workhorse. Sequence them rather than choosing one.

How do you personalize B2B SaaS outreach without sounding automated?

Treat personalization as proof of attention, not a merge token. Naming the company or the funding round is what automated sequences already do. Reference something that took effort to find: a product detail, a docs page, a hiring pattern, a bug. The measured gap is large: roughly 17-18% replies with advanced personalization against 7-9% without, in Woodpecker's data. The strongest form is proof of work, where the message carries something actually built for that prospect; that is demo-led outbound.

How many cold emails should a SaaS founder send per week?

As many as you can research properly, which is fewer than most plans assume. At about 30 minutes per prospect for real research and writing, two focused hours a day is about 20 prospects a week. Raising volume by cutting research usually lowers replies faster than it raises sends. The calendar math, and the options when it stops scaling, are on the founder-led sales page.

Do you need an AI SDR tool to do B2B SaaS outreach?

No. The first hundred sends teach you your ICP and your message, and a founder can run those by hand with a spreadsheet, a verified list, and their own inbox. Tools matter once the motion works and the constraint is hours.
