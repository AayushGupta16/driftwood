Cold outbound benchmarks: cold email reply rates and LinkedIn accept rates

Every number on this page has a named source and a year. Most benchmark roundups do not say where their numbers come from or how they were measured, which is how the same "average cold email reply rate" gets quoted as 1% and 9% in the same week. This page collects the public studies that publish their datasets, is honest about where they disagree, and adds one first-party dataset of our own, with methodology.

Average cold email reply rate

The public studies that publish their data:

StudyDatasetNumber
Sopro, State of Prospecting (2026) Sopro's outreach dataset across 82 industries 5.1% average response rate; most campaigns land between 1% and 5%
Belkins (2026) 7,530,489 client emails sent January to December 2025 0.45% average reply rate, counting replies against every email sent, follow-ups included; founders and owners replied at 0.57%, companies under 10 people at 0.72%
Woodpecker (2026) 20M+ cold emails sent through its platform advanced personalization replies at roughly 17-18%, against 7-9% without
Instantly (2026) benchmark guide, not a single dataset good = 5-10%, excellent = 10-15%, best-in-class = 15%+ on tight segments
Backlinko and Pitchbox (2019) 12 million outreach emails 8.5% received a response; note this was link-building and PR outreach, not sales
Belkins (2023) 11 million client emails across 90+ industries 7% average reply rate

Why these numbers disagree

They measure different things. Belkins' 0.45% divides replies by every email sent, follow-ups included, across a full year of at-scale agency campaigns. Sopro's 5.1% is a per-prospect response rate. Backlinko's 8.5% is from 2019 and covers link-building outreach, where reply norms differ from sales. Even Belkins against itself, 7% in 2023 versus 0.45% on 2025 data, reflects both a changed measurement and a channel that got much more crowded.

The honest summary: at average quality and at scale, cold email replies now measure under 1%. Small, carefully targeted, heavily personalized campaigns still measure between 5% and 18%. The spread between those two numbers is the entire game.

Average cold email open rate

Belkins measured a 36% average open rate across 11 million B2B cold emails in its 2023 study. That is the last clean public number worth quoting, because open rates have stopped meaning much: Apple Mail Privacy Protection has preloaded messages since 2021 and inflates opens for a large share of inboxes, and Belkins disabled open tracking entirely in 2025, so its 2026 study reports no open rate at all.

Treat opens as directional. If you want to know whether your email is being seen, run a placement test (covered below); it answers the question opens used to answer, without the tracking pixel.

LinkedIn connection accept rates and reply rates

StudyDatasetNumber
Expandi, State of LinkedIn Outreach (2026) 70,130+ campaigns on its platform 29.61% average connection accept rate; 10.3% average message reply rate
Belkins and Expandi (2025 data, published 2025) 15.1M+ outreach touchpoints, January to December 2025 18.7% of invited prospects accepted; 7.2% message reply rate; 1.3% of connected prospects booked a meeting

So the public range for cold connection accepts is roughly 19-30%, and LinkedIn message replies run about double cold email replies in the same datasets. One caveat on both studies: they come from automation platforms, so they measure automated outreach, which is the most template-heavy kind. Hand-picked targeting with a specific reason to connect sits above these numbers.

How many cold emails to book a meeting

Belkins' 2026 study reports over 1,200 appointments across 7.5 million emails sent in 2025, roughly one appointment per 6,000 emails. Its 2023 study reported about 306 cold emails per lead, under a looser definition of lead. The definitions differ, but the direction is the same: at average message quality, meetings per email sent are tiny, and they are getting tinier.

Public data on meeting rates is the weakest of any number on this page. Almost nobody publishes meetings-per-send with a definition of what counted as a meeting, and agency data mixes wildly different client quality. Read any meetings-per-email claim you find, including these, as one shop's observed rate, not a law.

Disclosure: driftwood's founder was a founding engineer at Autosana before building driftwood, and Autosana was driftwood's first customer. This is one company, one list, one week; it is an observed result, not a promise.

Methodology

What was sent
Cold outreach messages over LinkedIn and email. Every message carried a working demo of Autosana's product, built for the specific prospect receiving it; in several cases the demo showed a real bug the agent had found in the prospect's own app. The campaign sent 200 messages in total; the headline reply rate is from the first week of those sends.
Who received it
The same lead list Autosana's founders had been contacting with conventional founder-written outreach before the switch. No list change between the baseline and the demo-led week.
Time window
A three-week campaign in mid-2026, 200 messages across the three weeks. The under-1%-to-over-14% comparison is against week one of the campaign.
What counted as a reply
A human reply from the prospect to the outreach, on either channel, positive or negative. Bounces, auto-replies, and out-of-office messages did not count.
The baseline
Under 1%, the founders' self-reported reply rate on that list with their prior outreach.
What changed between the baseline and the demo-led week
Only the message contents and who produced them. driftwood's agent did the prospect research and built each demo; a human reviewed every message before it went out; sends came from the founders' own LinkedIn and email accounts, as before. Same product, same list, same senders.
Result
Replies went from under 1% to over 14% in week one at Autosana (YC S25), including founders who had ignored more than four months of prior outreach. One reply arrived about 12 hours after a message that opened with a real bug found on the prospect's own site, next to a 19-second demo video.

Reply rate below 1%? Check in this order

Most sub-1% campaigns are not a copy problem. Diagnose in this order, and do not skip ahead:

  1. Bounce rate first. If more than 2% of your sends bounce, stop and fix the list before anything else; Woodpecker's benchmark from 20M+ sends is under 2% good, under 1.5% excellent. High bounces mean stale or guessed addresses, and they poison sender reputation, which drags every later send down. Verify every address before it enters a campaign.
  2. Placement test second. A reply rate means nothing if the emails sit in spam. Run a seed-list placement test (GlockApps, MailReach, or similar) and fix what it finds. Since 2024, Google's sender guidelines require SPF, DKIM, and DMARC for bulk senders and expect user-reported spam under 0.1%, never above 0.3%. Sopro's 2026 benchmark for healthy deliverability is 95-98%. Until you place in the inbox, copy changes are noise.
  3. Only then, copy and targeting. If bounces are under 2% and placement is clean, the message is the problem. The biggest measured lever is how much real, prospect-specific work the message shows: Woodpecker's data has advanced personalization at roughly 17-18% replies against 7-9% without. Our data says the strongest form of that is not personalized copy but personalized proof, a working demo of your product built for their business; that is demo-led outbound.

Common questions

What is a good cold email reply rate?

Instantly's 2026 benchmark guide calls 5-10% good for B2B teams, 10-15% excellent, and 15%+ best-in-class on tightly segmented campaigns. Sopro's State of Prospecting 2026 puts most campaigns between 1% and 5%. If you are below 1%, work the diagnostic order above before touching the copy.

What is the average cold email response rate?

It depends on how you count. Sopro (2026) reports 5.1% on a per-prospect basis. Belkins (2026) measured 0.45% across 7,530,489 emails sent in 2025 when replies are divided by every email sent, follow-ups included. Backlinko and Pitchbox (2019) found 8.5%, on link-building outreach rather than sales. There is no single average; there are averages per measurement method.

What is the average cold email open rate?

Belkins measured 36% across 11 million B2B cold emails in 2023. Treat it as directional: Apple Mail Privacy Protection has inflated opens since 2021, and Belkins dropped open tracking entirely in 2025. A placement test is the better instrument now.

What is a good LinkedIn connection request acceptance rate?

The public platform studies put the average between roughly 19% and 30%: Expandi (2026) measured 29.61% across 70,000+ campaigns, and the Belkins and Expandi joint study of 2025 data measured 18.7%. Below about 20%, the usual cause is targeting, not the connection note.

How many cold emails does it take to book a meeting?

At average quality, thousands. Belkins (2026) booked over 1,200 appointments across 7.5 million emails, roughly one per 6,000 sent; its 2023 study reported about 306 emails per lead under a looser definition. The lever is quality per message, not volume.

Do cold emails still work in 2026?

Yes, but the average keeps falling while the top end holds. Belkins measured 0.45% average replies on 2025 sends; Woodpecker's data shows advanced personalization replying at roughly 17-18%. The channel works; templated volume does not. Our own numbers point the same way: replies went from under 1% to over 14% in week one at Autosana (YC S25) when every message carried a working demo. How do I improve my cold email response rate?

In order: bounce rate under 2%, placement test until you land in the inbox, then the message. Once delivery is clean, show real work in the message; the measured gap between advanced personalization and none is roughly 17-18% versus 7-9% (Woodpecker).
