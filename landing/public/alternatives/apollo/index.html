What Apollo is, and what it is genuinely good at

Apollo is a sales platform built around a B2B contact database that Apollo says covers more than 240 million contacts and 30 million companies as of July 2026. Around the database sits most of the outbound stack: email sequencing, a dialer, meeting scheduling, enrichment, intent topics, light CRM and deal features, and an AI assistant. It is one per-seat product that replaces what would otherwise be a data provider, a sequencer, and a dialer bought separately.

As of July 2026 there is a free tier with a small monthly credit grant, then three paid plans billed annually: Basic at $49 per user per month, Professional at $79, and Organization at $119 with a three seat minimum. Monthly billing costs more. Apollo restructured its credit system in late 2025: paid plans now grant a yearly credit pool per seat, 30,000 on Basic, 48,000 on Professional, and 72,000 on Organization, which covers contact reveals and exports. Phone numbers consume several times the credits of an email, and credits do not roll over.

What it is genuinely good at: consolidation at a low price. One login covers finding contacts, sequencing them, and calling them, and $49 a seat undercuts buying a database and a sequencer separately by a wide margin. The free tier is a real evaluation path, coverage of US tech and SMB companies is strong, and because Apollo is everywhere, most reps you hire will already know it. As the pragmatic default for a small US-focused sales team, it is hard to argue with.

Where Apollo falls short, and for whom

Data accuracy depends on your segment

Coverage of US tech and SMB is Apollo's home turf. Reviews and Reddit threads through 2026 report staler data outside it: outdated titles and bounced emails are the most common complaints for Europe, APAC, and Latin America. The pattern is consistent enough that you should verify exported lists with an independent checker before sending, whatever your segment. If your market is one Apollo covers thinly, the database that justifies the bundle stops justifying it.

The credit meter

Since the late 2025 restructure, usage is metered against a yearly credit pool per seat. Reveal-heavy prospecting burns through it, phone numbers cost several times what emails do, and unused credits expire. Teams that prospect hard on lower tiers report hitting the pool well before the year ends, at which point the choice is overage credits or a tier upgrade. Budget for how you actually prospect, not the sticker.

Deliverability is your problem, not Apollo's

The workflow Apollo makes easiest, exporting a filtered list straight into a sequence, is also the fastest way to burn a sending domain. The verified label on a contact is not a guarantee, and 2026 reviews regularly describe damaged domains from teams that trusted it and skipped their own checks. Apollo ships warmup and deliverability guidance, but placement is still yours to earn, on your own domains.

Everything included, nothing best-in-class

The bundle is the pitch, and each piece shows it. The sequencer has fewer sending and infrastructure controls than dedicated senders, the dialer trails purpose-built dialers, and the CRM features are light enough that most teams still run a real CRM beside it. Teams tend to outgrow Apollo one piece at a time, which is exactly how the alternatives below are organized.

More sends will not fix low replies

Apollo makes it easy to put thousands of people into a templated sequence, and for many teams that is the whole strategy. If replies are under 1 percent, the platform is not the bottleneck; the message is. Switching to a different database and a different sequencer to send the same message rarely moves the number.

2

Amplemarket

Amplemarket is the closest like-for-like swap: contact data, multichannel engagement, deliverability tooling, and an AI copilot called Duo in one platform. It keeps the consolidation trade that brought you to Apollo but aims it at sales teams willing to pay more for better tooling around deliverability and messaging. If the bundle is right and the execution is what disappoints, evaluate this one first.

Best for: sales teams that want data and outreach from one vendor at a higher grade than Apollo's.

3

Clay

If your complaint is specifically data quality, Clay is the direct answer. Instead of one database, it runs waterfall enrichment across more than a hundred providers, checking each in turn until one has the contact, plus AI research columns and buying signals. As of July 2026 there is a free tier and paid plans from $185 a month ($167 billed annually). The trade is real: Clay is a workbench that wants an operator, not a database with a search box. We compared Clay alternatives separately.

Best for: teams whose bottleneck is data coverage or accuracy, with someone technical to own the workbench.

4

Smartlead

Smartlead replaces the sequencer, not the database: dedicated cold email infrastructure with unlimited mailboxes and warmup included, from $39 a month as of July 2026. Teams that keep Apollo for data on a cheap tier and move sending to Smartlead get better deliverability control than Apollo's sequencer offers, at the cost of running two tools.

Best for: teams keeping their data source but moving sending onto dedicated infrastructure.

5

lemlist

lemlist covers the channel Apollo mostly does not: email and LinkedIn steps in one sequence, with calling on higher tiers, plus its own lead database. Pricing is per user, $69 a month for email only and $109 for multichannel as of July 2026. If your prospects answer LinkedIn DMs and ignore cold email, this is the shape of tool to switch to.

Best for: small teams whose prospects live on LinkedIn as much as email.

6

Instantly

Instantly is the budget volume answer: sending plans from $47 a month as of July 2026 with unlimited mailboxes and warmup included, and its own lead database as a separate add-on. Against Apollo the case is pure sending economics at high volume; the data quality caveats that apply to Apollo apply at least as much here. We compared Instantly alternatives separately.

Best for: teams whose model is high-volume cold email at the lowest credible price.

When Apollo is the right choice

More often than any other tool we cover, Apollo is the right choice. If you sell into US companies, run a normal per-seat sales team, and want data, sequencing, and calling in one product, nothing on this page beats Apollo's price-to-coverage ratio, and most of the alternatives cost more in total once you rebuild the bundle from parts. The complaints above are real, but they are the cost of consolidation, and for many teams the convenience is worth it. The honest dividing line: keep Apollo while the bundle is the value. Move when one specific failure, data in your segment, deliverability, or a reply rate that will not budge, costs you more than the convenience saves, and then pick the alternative aimed at that failure rather than a second bundle.
