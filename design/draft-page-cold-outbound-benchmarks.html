What cold email reply rates typically look like

The honest version first: there is no audited industry dataset for cold email. The figures below are the ranges most commonly cited across vendor reports and practitioner writeups as of mid-2026. We did not produce them, we cannot verify them, and the people publishing numbers like these usually sell outreach software. Treat them as the backdrop, not the ground truth.

Two caveats worth more than the table. Open rates have been unreliable since inbox privacy features started loading tracking pixels automatically; a recorded open is often not a person reading. And averages hide the split that matters: the gap between generic volume outreach and outreach that carries something specific is much larger than the gap between any two tools sending the same kind of message.

Why most cold outbound underperforms

The commonly cited ranges are low for reasons that show up in almost every underperforming campaign. Three patterns account for most of it.

Volume first

Tooling made sends nearly free, so the default strategy became more sends. Most AI SDR tools compete on exactly this. But volume shows up in dashboards, not in replies, and it carries costs the dashboard does not show: burned sending domains, mail landing in spam, and lead lists that stop being usable.

Thin personalization

A merge tag fills in the first name, a scraped detail supplies the opener, and a language model restates the sender's homepage in a friendly tone. It costs almost nothing to produce, and it reads that way. Buyers pattern-match the style and delete it unread.

No proof in the message

The typical cold message asks for time and offers claims. There is nothing in it the prospect can check in ten seconds, so the claims are worth what they cost to make: nothing. A prospect who cannot verify anything has no reason to reply, and the reply rates above reflect that.

What moved the number

The Autosana campaign changed one variable: what the message carried. The mechanics, from the case study:

  1. For each prospect on the existing list, the agent researched the company and built a working demo of Autosana for their business: Autosana's agent running against the prospect's own product.
  2. The message opened with something checkable. In the published thread, that was a real bug the agent found on the prospect's own site, with a 19 second video of Autosana's agent catching it.
  3. A human approved every message before it sent. Nothing left the queue on its own.
  4. Everything sent from the founder's own accounts, LinkedIn or email, so replies landed where he already works.

This approach has a name, demo-led outbound, with a full definition on its own page. The short version of why it moves reply rates: the message gives the prospect proof of work they can verify before they spend any time, which is the one thing volume outreach cannot fake.

Frequently asked questions

Do cold emails still work?

Yes, with a qualifier: the typical version works poorly. Commonly cited reply rates sit in the low single digits, and untargeted blasts are commonly reported under 1%. What still works is outreach that carries something the prospect can verify. Run that way, replies went from under 1% to over 14% in week one at Autosana (YC S25), on a lead list that had been ignoring generic outreach for months.

What is a good cold email reply rate?

Commonly cited figures put typical cold email reply rates between 1% and 5%, with anything above 5% usually described as good. Those are industry numbers we cannot verify. Our own data point, from the Autosana case study: replies went from under 1% to over 14% in week one at Autosana (YC S25), after switching the same lead list to demo-led outbound.

What is the average cold email open rate?

Typically reported ranges run from about 20% to 40%, and we would not lean on any of them. Open tracking broke when inbox privacy features started loading tracking pixels automatically, so a recorded open is often not a person reading. driftwood does not publish open rates for that reason; we benchmark on replies, which a mail client cannot fake.

How many cold emails does it take to book a meeting?

At the commonly cited rates above, the arithmetic lands in the hundreds of sends per booked meeting for typical volume campaigns. We do not yet publish a meetings-per-send benchmark of our own; the sample is too small to call it one. The data point we can publish: in the Autosana campaign, one message to a CTO who had ignored 4.5 months of outreach drew a reply in about 12 hours, and the call was booked three days after the send.
